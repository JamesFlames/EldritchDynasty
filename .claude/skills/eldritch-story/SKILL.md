# Eldritch story design

Architecture, and the voice of the frame. Full manual: `reference/story-manual.md` — go to the section you need.

**What this skill owns:** overall arcs, Age structure, the escalation, the cosmic tier, and the *prose* of the frame, interludes, prologue, Ledger clauses, nested tales and Age blurbs — all of which are written in **Dunsanian register**.

**What it does not own:** individual event bodies, outcome text and character blurbs. Those are **pure Rothfuss** — use **rothfuss-prose**. Do not apply elevated diction to them.

## Three traditions, three jobs

**Rothfuss builds the house, Dunsany decides what the light is like in it, Lovecraft decides what is underneath it.**

| Tradition | Owns |
|---|---|
| **Rothfuss** | The three layers, Ages-as-episodes, the three debts, cast slots, tension engines, ledger discipline, human-scale stakes, the legend/truth gap |
| **Dunsany** | Register of the frame and myth layer. Beauty before dread. Melancholy. Mythic distance. Motif |
| **Lovecraft** | The cosmic tier, epistemic escalation, knowledge-as-danger, what is never explained |

Four points where they genuinely conflict, resolved in §1. **Do not re-open these mid-design.**

## The two sentences everything hangs off

Structural — write this first, and make it a sentence rather than a theme:

> *This is the true story of how the house became the thing people sing about — told by the last of the line, on the last night, to the creditor sitting across the table.*

Tonal:

> *A beautiful myth, told by someone who has glimpsed something behind the myth.*

If a feature does not touch **blood, the ledger, or the record**, it is not in this game. If a scene has dread but no beauty, it is half-built.

## Three layers, and do not let them blur

| Layer | What | Volume | Tempo | Register |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Frame** | 2042. Two at a table, and the chronicle between them | ~5% | Slower than everything around it | **Dunsanian** |
| **The Tale** | 1042–2042. The run itself, in the chronicler's voice | ~90% | A generation is a chapter | **Rothfuss** — carries the warmth |
| **Nested tales** | What the world says about the family | ~5% | Interruptions | **Dunsanian** |

The frame is the **promise**: every generation is played against *how does this become that?* It reacts to the record and never dispenses systems information.

The register split is a feature. When the prose goes ornate, the player has left the ledger and entered the myth — and the myth is the layer that lies.

## Designing a phase

An Age is a phase, and a phase that shares all its content with every other phase is a modifier wearing a name. **Every Age pays three debts** before it is authored:

1. **One Ledger clause** — a hard fact about the contract, not a hint. What *kind* of fact is set by the Age's escalation stage.
2. **One standing change** — the family ends materially richer, poorer, more feared or more exposed.
3. **One Rumour** — a story about the family entering the world distorted.

An Age delivering none of these is a beautiful two hundred years in which nothing happened, and that is precisely the drift long games die of.

Three scheduling rules: **name Ages late**, **alternate the register** (warm/prosperous → cold/lethal → institutional/political), and **never pay the same kind of clause twice running**.

## The escalation — one stage per Age

Seven stages across nine Ages. The stage governs the Age's intensity and what its clause is allowed to say.

| Age | Stage | The clause |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wonder | A contract exists, and has terms. Favourable-seeming |
| 2 | Mystery | The family's account of the signing is incomplete |
| 3–4 | Wrongness | A term that does not behave like a term; then a second, related to the first |
| 5 | Revelation | The other party predates the family, the house, the contract's language |
| 6–7 | Incomprehension | A reading held for centuries is invalidated; then the correction is |
| 8 | Cosmic implication | This contract is one of many, or was never the point |
| 9 | Silence | **No clause.** Permitted only if the other eight paid |

Incomprehension is the hardest stage and the one that justifies the structure — it requires the player to have been confidently wrong for two hundred years, which requires a genuinely *good* wrong answer (manual §11).

## Two tiers of antagonist

**Tier A — the proximate mythic.** Tragic, knowable in principle, appears once catastrophically, denied by educated society, known by **displacement and omen** — iron rusting overnight, dogs that will not enter a room they have slept in for years. The early game is not about beating it; it is about establishing that it exists, against an institution that would ruin you for saying so.

**Tier B — the cosmic.** The other party. Indifferent, no stated motive, never explained. Design it by answering six questions in your notes (manual §7), of which *what remains impossible to understand* must be the largest.

**The seam is where the horror is.** The ancestor who signed believed he was **bargaining with someone**. Everything the family has done since rests on the assumption that the other party has interests that can be satisfied. Nothing ever confirms it.

Never give Tier B a line like *"I shall have what I am owed."* Prefer: *The seal on the box had not been broken, and the box was open.*

## Cast slots, not characters

Write to **roles that get refilled** — the Mentor Taken Away, the Institutional Rival, the Fragile One, the Unattainable — so a thousand years rhymes without an author wiring it. A recurring role whose occupant dies and is not recast silently removes every scene that referenced it.

## Nested tales

**No nested tale is neutral.** Every one is told by someone with a stake, and the teller is always named and always biased. Any event of consequence has at least two surviving accounts **that disagree**, and the game never adjudicates in its own voice. Two accounts that agree are one account written twice.

## Beauty before dread

The world must possess beauty before it possesses dread, and **the beautiful should be temporary** — a hall only ever full once in a generation, a garden that blooms in a year nobody can afford to enjoy it, a great house being slowly forgotten by the country around it.

Beauty is not a break from the horror. It is what gives the horror somewhere to land, and the reason the player minds. Every Age needs one thing the player would mind losing.

## The two-class ledger

Every question is classified the day it opens, and the classification is permanent.

**Class M — mundane.** Written answer, partial payoff within an Age, hard expiry, **closed on schedule and the player is told.** At least one closes per Age, including Age 9. Cap six live.

**Class C — cosmic.** Written answer **before the vertical slice**, and never shipped. Payoffs **eliminate rather than confirm** — each clause removes readings, never states what the thing is. Never closed, ever. Cap two live.

Expiry works differently for Class C: a cosmic question dies not from being unanswered but from going **un-narrowed**. Two consecutive Ages with no eliminating clause and the player has stopped believing in it.

**Reclassification is forbidden.** A cosmic question cannot be demoted late because the ending needs tidying.

## Before calling a design finished

Run the full checklist (manual §22). The ones this project has actually hit:

- **Spine stall** — chart clause recovery per 200 years. A flat stretch is a redesign, not a tuning pass.
- **Formidability spam** — count the scenes whose real job is *the family is impressive*. More than one per Age is too many.
- **A frame with no function** — does it complicate the record, or just look mysterious?
- **Unwritten ending** — Tier B's true nature must exist in full before the vertical slice.
- **The explained god** — does the entity anywhere have a motive, a plan, or a grievance?
- **No good wrong answer** — if truth Layer 2 is a strawman, the Incomprehension stage has nothing to break.
- **Nihilism** — no cosmic revelation may make an earlier human loss trivial in the game's own voice.
- **Register bleed** — Dunsanian diction in event bodies, or plain reportage in the frame.
