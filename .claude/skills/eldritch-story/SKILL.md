# Rothfuss story design

Architecture, not sentences. Full manual: `reference/story-manual.md` — go to the section you need.

Sentence-level craft is a different skill: **rothfuss-prose**.

## The one thing everything hangs off

Write the **thesis sentence** first, and make it a sentence rather than a theme. This game's is:

> *This is the true story of how the house became the thing people sing about — told by the last of the line, on the last night, to the creditor sitting across the table.*

Every phase, Age and arc is downstream of it. If a feature does not touch **blood, the ledger, or the record**, it is not in this game.

## Three layers, and do not let them blur

| Layer | What | Volume | Tempo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Frame** | 2042. Two at a table, and the chronicle between them | ~5% | Slower than everything around it |
| **The Tale** | 1042–2042. The run itself, in the chronicler's voice | ~90% | A generation is a chapter |
| **Nested tales** | What the world says about the family | ~5% | Interruptions |

The frame is the **promise**: every generation is played against *how does this become that?* It reacts to the record and never dispenses systems information.

## Designing a phase

An Age is a phase, and a phase that shares all its content with every other phase is a modifier wearing a name. **Every Age must pay three debts** before it is authored:

1. **One Ledger clause** — a hard fact about the contract, not a hint.
2. **One standing change** — the family ends materially richer, poorer, more feared or more exposed.
3. **One Rumour** — a story about the family entering the world distorted.

An Age delivering none of these is a beautiful two hundred years in which nothing happened, and that is precisely the drift long games die of.

Two scheduling rules: **name Ages late** (the player feels two decades of effects first), and **alternate the register** — never two of the same texture consecutively. Warm/prosperous → cold/lethal → institutional/political.

## The spine pays out on a schedule

Opening a thousand-year mystery and paying nothing until the last hour is **promise debt**, and it is the largest structural risk in a long game. The fix is mechanical: nine clauses, one per Age, each a hard fact that changes what is possible at the end. **The answers are written before anything ships.**

## The mythic antagonist

On screen twice in a thousand years. Known by **displacement and omen** rather than description — iron rusting overnight, dogs that will not enter a room they have slept in for years. Denied by educated society. The early game is not about beating it; it is about establishing that it exists, against an institution that would ruin you for saying so.

## Cast slots, not characters

Write to **roles that get refilled** — the Tutor, the Rival, the Fragile One, the Match That Never Comes — so a thousand years rhymes without an author wiring it. A recurring role whose occupant dies and is not recast silently removes every scene that referenced it.

## Nested tales

**No nested tale is neutral.** Every one is told by someone with a stake, and the teller is always named and always biased. Any event of consequence has at least two surviving accounts **that disagree**, and the game never adjudicates in its own voice. Two accounts that agree are one account written twice.

## Before calling a design finished

Run the failure-mode checklist (manual §19). The ones this project has actually hit:

- **Spine stall** — chart clause recovery per 200 years. A flat stretch is a redesign, not a tuning pass.
- **Formidability spam** — count the scenes whose real job is *the family is impressive*. More than one per Age is too many.
- **A frame with no function** — does it complicate the tale, or just look mysterious?
- **Unwritten ending** — the antagonist's true nature must exist before the vertical slice.
